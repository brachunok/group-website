What is every assistant professor searching for? More obligations!

In the spirit of trying new things, this is the official beginning of the research group blog. In a world of LinkedIn lunatics posting things like [“What I learned by creating a pull request during my wedding ceremony”](https://www.businessinsider.com/linkedin-posting-go-direct-ai-slop-hiring-2026-9), I want to try a few bouts of old-school writing.

I was fortunate to grow up in what I think was the golden age of internet content: when blogs were just put out there for the love of the game. I remember being thrilled when Google Reader (RIP) told me that one of my favorite RSS feeds had been updated. There were tech blogs like the original [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/), bike blogs like [Bike Snob NYC](https://bikesnobnyc.com/) and [Prolly Is Not Probably](https://johnprolly.blogspot.com/), and even groups of friends writing about the pranks they pulled, like [All Too Flat](http://www.alltooflat.com/). The tone was fun, adventurous, and a reflection of what I think of as the old internet, where online interaction was an extension of in-person life rather than whatever version of that we have today.

In grad school and as a postdoc, it was a joy to read the newest posts from [Laura Albert](https://punkrockor.com/), [Matt Might](https://matt.might.net/), [Pat Reed’s group](https://waterprogramming.wordpress.com/), and [Statistics by Jim](https://statisticsbyjim.com/). It was equally fun to stumble across articles like Radhika Nagpal’s [“The Awesomest 7-Year Postdoc”](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/guest-blog/the-awesomest-7-year-postdoc-or-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-tenure-track-faculty-life/), Richard Hamming’s writing and stories about setting aside time for [“great thoughts”](https://medium.com/@Jake_Color/what-i-learned-from-richard-hamming-6b668d19583c), or one of a million Richard Feynman anecdotes that were probably made up.

Seeing people enjoy what they did, share little tips with one another, and give some humanity to a world that could otherwise feel a little cold was an absolute treat. Those posts could reliably provide a spark to help me keep working, try something new, or just think a little differently.

So, on behalf of a website that is half pay-it-forward and half engagement economy: welcome to the blog. You'll hear from me (Ben), grad students, undergrads, and maybe even guests from time to time. We hope it's fun.
